WORKPLACE HEALTH AND SAFETY ACT 2024

No. 28 of 2024

An Act to secure the health, safety, and welfare of workers and
other persons at work, to eliminate or minimise risks arising from
work, and for related purposes.

The Parliament of Queensland enacts:

PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

Section 1 — Short title
    This Act may be cited as the Workplace Health and Safety Act 2024.

Section 2 — Commencement
    This Act commences on 1 January 2025.

Section 3 — Object
    The object of this Act is to provide for a balanced and nationally
    consistent framework to secure the health and safety of workers
    and workplaces by—
    (a) protecting workers and other persons against harm to their
        health, safety, and welfare through the elimination or
        minimisation of risks arising from work; and
    (b) providing for fair and effective workplace representation,
        consultation, cooperation, and issue resolution in relation
        to work health and safety; and
    (c) encouraging unions and employer organisations to take a
        constructive role in promoting improvements in work health
        and safety practices; and
    (d) promoting the provision of advice, information, education,
        and training in relation to work health and safety; and
    (e) securing compliance through effective and appropriate
        enforcement measures.

Section 4 — Definitions
    In this Act—
    "PCBU" means a person conducting a business or undertaking,
        whether alone or with others and whether or not for profit;
    "worker" means a person who carries out work in any capacity for
        a PCBU, including as an employee, contractor, subcontractor,
        outworker, apprentice, trainee, work experience student, or
        volunteer;
    "workplace" means a place where work is carried out for a business
        or undertaking and includes any place where a worker goes, or
        is likely to be, while at work;
    "notifiable incident" has the meaning given by section 35;
    "health and safety representative" means a worker elected under
        Part 5 to represent workers on health and safety matters;
    "inspector" means a person appointed as an inspector under Part 8;
    "reasonably practicable" means that which is, or was at a particular
        time, reasonably able to be done in relation to ensuring health
        and safety, taking into account—
        (a) the likelihood of the hazard or risk occurring; and
        (b) the degree of harm that might result; and
        (c) what the person knows or ought reasonably to know; and
        (d) the availability and suitability of ways to eliminate or
            minimise the risk; and
        (e) the cost of eliminating or minimising the risk.

PART 2 — HEALTH AND SAFETY DUTIES

Division 1 — Duties of PCBUs

Section 5 — Primary duty of care
    (1) A PCBU must ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, the
        health and safety of—
        (a) workers engaged, or caused to be engaged, by the PCBU; and
        (b) workers whose activities in carrying out work are influenced
            or directed by the PCBU;
        while the workers are at work in the business or undertaking.
    (2) A PCBU must ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, that
        the health and safety of other persons is not put at risk from
        work carried out as part of the conduct of the business or
        undertaking.
    (3) Without limiting subsections (1) and (2), a PCBU must ensure,
        so far as is reasonably practicable—
        (a) the provision and maintenance of a work environment without
            risks to health and safety; and
        (b) the provision and maintenance of safe plant and structures; and
        (c) the provision and maintenance of safe systems of work; and
        (d) the safe use, handling, and storage of plant, structures,
            and substances; and
        (e) the provision of adequate facilities for the welfare of
            workers; and
        (f) the provision of information, training, instruction, or
            supervision necessary to protect all persons from risks; and
        (g) the monitoring of the health of workers and the conditions
            at the workplace for the purpose of preventing illness or
            injury.
    (4) Cross-reference: for the interaction with the Road Safety Act 2024
        regarding automated driving system entities, see section 16 of
        that Act.

Section 6 — Duty in relation to substances
    A PCBU must ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, that—
    (a) hazardous substances are correctly labelled; and
    (b) safety data sheets are available for all hazardous substances; and
    (c) workers who handle hazardous substances receive appropriate training.

Section 7 — Duty to manage risks
    (1) A PCBU must manage risks to health and safety by—
        (a) eliminating risks so far as is reasonably practicable; and
        (b) if it is not reasonably practicable to eliminate risks—
            minimising those risks so far as is reasonably practicable
            by implementing control measures in accordance with
            subsection (2).
    (2) The hierarchy of control measures is—
        (a) substitution;
        (b) isolation;
        (c) engineering controls;
        (d) administrative controls;
        (e) personal protective equipment.

Division 2 — Duties of Officers

Section 8 — Duty of officers
    If a PCBU owes a duty under this Part, an officer of the PCBU must
    exercise due diligence to ensure that the PCBU complies with that
    duty, including by—
    (a) acquiring and keeping up-to-date knowledge of work health and
        safety matters; and
    (b) understanding the nature of operations and the hazards and
        risks associated with those operations; and
    (c) ensuring that appropriate resources and processes are available
        to eliminate or minimise risks; and
    (d) ensuring that processes for receiving and considering information
        regarding incidents, hazards, and risks are in place; and
    (e) ensuring compliance with the PCBU's duties and obligations.

Division 3 — Duties of Workers

Section 9 — Duties of workers
    While at work, a worker must—
    (a) take reasonable care for their own health and safety; and
    (b) take reasonable care that their acts or omissions do not
        adversely affect the health and safety of other persons; and
    (c) comply, so far as is reasonably able, with any reasonable
        instruction given by the PCBU; and
    (d) cooperate with any reasonable policy or procedure of the PCBU.

PART 3 — CONSULTATION, REPRESENTATION, AND PARTICIPATION

Division 1 — Consultation Requirements

Section 10 — Duty to consult workers
    (1) A PCBU must, so far as is reasonably practicable, consult with
        workers who carry out work for the business or undertaking and
        who are, or are likely to be, directly affected by a matter
        relating to work health or safety.
    (2) Consultation is required in relation to—
        (a) identifying hazards and assessing risks arising from work; and
        (b) making decisions about ways to eliminate or minimise risks; and
        (c) making decisions about the adequacy of facilities; and
        (d) proposing changes that may affect the health or safety of
            workers; and
        (e) making decisions about procedures for consultation,
            monitoring, or providing information or training.
    (3) Consultation must involve—
        (a) sharing relevant information about the matter with workers; and
        (b) giving workers a reasonable opportunity to express their
            views; and
        (c) taking those views into account; and
        (d) advising workers of the outcome of consultation.

Section 11 — Consultation with other PCBUs
    If more than one PCBU has a duty in relation to the same matter,
    each PCBU must, so far as is reasonably practicable, consult,
    cooperate, and coordinate activities with each other PCBU.

Division 2 — Health and Safety Representatives

Section 12 — Election of representatives
    (1) Workers at a workplace may request the PCBU to facilitate the
        election of one or more health and safety representatives.
    (2) The PCBU must facilitate the election within 14 days.
    (3) A health and safety representative holds office for 3 years
        unless they cease to be a worker at the workplace.

Section 13 — Powers of representatives
    A health and safety representative for a work group may—
    (a) represent workers in the group on matters relating to work
        health and safety; and
    (b) monitor measures taken by the PCBU in compliance with this Act; and
    (c) investigate complaints from workers regarding health and safety; and
    (d) issue a provisional improvement notice under section 34.

PART 4 — NOTIFIABLE INCIDENTS

Section 14 — Meaning of notifiable incident
    A notifiable incident means—
    (a) the death of a person; or
    (b) a serious injury or illness of a person; or
    (c) a dangerous incident.

Section 15 — Meaning of serious injury or illness
    A serious injury or illness means an injury or illness requiring—
    (a) immediate treatment as an in-patient in a hospital; or
    (b) immediate treatment for—
        (i) the amputation of any part of the body; or
        (ii) a serious head injury; or
        (iii) a serious eye injury; or
        (iv) a serious burn; or
        (v) the separation of skin from underlying tissue; or
        (vi) a spinal injury; or
        (vii) loss of a bodily function; or
        (viii) serious lacerations.

Section 16 — Meaning of dangerous incident
    A dangerous incident means an incident in relation to a workplace
    that exposes a worker or any other person to a serious risk to
    health or safety, including—
    (a) an uncontrolled escape, spillage, or leakage of a substance; or
    (b) an uncontrolled implosion, explosion, or fire; or
    (c) an uncontrolled escape of gas or steam; or
    (d) the collapse or failure of an excavation, or of a structure; or
    (e) electric shock; or
    (f) the fall or release from a height of any plant, substance, or thing.

Section 17 — Duty to notify regulator
    (1) A PCBU must notify the regulator immediately after becoming
        aware that a notifiable incident arising out of the conduct of
        the business or undertaking has occurred.
    (2) The notice must be given by the fastest possible means.
    (3) The PCBU must ensure that the site is preserved until an
        inspector arrives or directs otherwise.
    (4) Penalty: 500 penalty units for a body corporate;
        100 penalty units for an individual.
    (5) Cross-reference: for inspector powers on site, see Part 8.

PART 5 — HEALTH AND SAFETY COMMITTEES

Section 18 — Establishment of committees
    (1) A PCBU must establish a health and safety committee within
        2 months after being requested to do so by—
        (a) a health and safety representative; or
        (b) 5 or more workers at the workplace.
    (2) A committee must consist of equal numbers of representatives
        nominated by the PCBU and elected by workers.

PART 6 — ISSUE RESOLUTION

Section 19 — Procedures for issue resolution
    (1) If an issue about work health or safety arises at a workplace,
        the parties must attempt to resolve it at the workplace level.
    (2) If the issue is not resolved, any party may request the
        assistance of an inspector under Part 8.

PART 7 — PENALTIES

Section 20 — Category 1 offences
    (1) A person commits a Category 1 offence if the person, without
        reasonable excuse, engages in conduct that exposes an individual
        to a risk of death or serious injury or illness and the person
        is reckless as to the risk.
    (2) Maximum penalty—
        (a) for a body corporate—30,000 penalty units;
        (b) for an officer—6,000 penalty units or 5 years imprisonment;
        (c) for any other person—3,000 penalty units or 5 years imprisonment.

Section 21 — Category 2 offences
    (1) A person commits a Category 2 offence if the person fails to
        comply with a health and safety duty and the failure exposes an
        individual to a risk of death or serious injury or illness.
    (2) Maximum penalty—
        (a) for a body corporate—15,000 penalty units;
        (b) for an officer—3,000 penalty units;
        (c) for any other person—1,500 penalty units.

PART 8 — ENFORCEMENT

Section 22 — Appointment of inspectors
    The regulator may appoint inspectors for the purposes of this Act.

Section 23 — Powers of inspectors
    An inspector may, for the purposes of this Act—
    (a) enter a workplace at any reasonable time;
    (b) inspect and examine any thing at the workplace;
    (c) require the production of documents;
    (d) take measurements, samples, and recordings;
    (e) interview any person at the workplace.

Section 24 — Improvement notices
    An inspector may issue an improvement notice if the inspector
    reasonably believes that a person is contravening or has contravened
    a provision of this Act.

Section 25 — Prohibition notices
    An inspector may issue a prohibition notice if the inspector
    reasonably believes that an activity is occurring or may occur at a
    workplace that involves a serious risk to the health or safety of
    a person.

PART 9 — MISCELLANEOUS

Section 26 — Regulations
    The Governor in Council may make regulations for or with respect to
    any matter necessary to give effect to this Act.

Section 27 — Review of Act
    The Minister must cause an independent review of this Act to be
    conducted within 5 years after commencement.
