ROAD SAFETY ACT 2024

No. 42 of 2024

An Act to promote road safety, regulate the use of motor vehicles,
and provide for the licensing of drivers in the State of Victoria.

The Parliament of Victoria enacts:

PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

Division 1 — Introduction

Section 1 — Short title
    This Act may be cited as the Road Safety Act 2024.

Section 2 — Commencement
    (1) This Act comes into operation on a day or days to be proclaimed.
    (2) If a provision of this Act does not come into operation before
        1 July 2025, it comes into operation on that day.

Section 3 — Purposes
    The purposes of this Act are—
    (a) to provide for the safe and efficient use of roads; and
    (b) to reduce road trauma; and
    (c) to establish a regulatory framework for automated vehicles; and
    (d) to provide for the licensing and registration of drivers and vehicles.

Section 4 — Definitions
    In this Act—
    "automated driving system" means the hardware and software that are
        collectively capable of performing the entire dynamic driving task
        on a sustained basis;
    "BAC" means blood alcohol concentration as defined in Part 3;
    "driver" means a person who drives a motor vehicle on a highway and
        includes a person who is learning to drive under section 24;
    "highway" has the same meaning as in the Transport Integration Act 2010;
    "motor vehicle" means a vehicle that uses an engine or motor for
        propulsion, but does not include a vehicle that—
        (a) cannot travel at more than 10 kilometres per hour; or
        (b) is a motorised wheelchair;
    "PCBU" has the meaning given in the Workplace Health and Safety Act 2024;
    "penalty unit" has the value fixed from time to time under the
        Monetary Units Act 2004;
    "registered operator" means the person in whose name a vehicle is
        registered under Part 5;
    "road" includes a highway, street, lane, or thoroughfare.

Division 2 — Application

Section 5 — Application of Act
    (1) This Act applies to all roads within Victoria.
    (2) This Act binds the Crown in right of the State of Victoria.
    (3) Nothing in this Act affects the operation of the Privacy Act 2024
        (Cth) with respect to the collection of personal information
        under this Act—see section 12 of the Privacy Act 2024.

PART 2 — DRIVER LICENSING

Division 1 — Learner Permits

Section 6 — Eligibility for learner permit
    A person may apply for a learner permit if the person—
    (a) has attained the age of 16 years; and
    (b) has passed the prescribed knowledge test; and
    (c) is a fit and proper person to hold a learner permit.

Section 7 — Conditions of learner permit
    (1) A learner permit is subject to the following conditions—
        (a) the holder must not drive a motor vehicle unless accompanied
            by a supervising driver as defined in section 8;
        (b) the holder must display L-plates in the prescribed manner;
        (c) the holder must not have a BAC exceeding 0.00.
    (2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence.
        Penalty: 10 penalty units.

Section 8 — Supervising driver
    A supervising driver must—
    (a) hold a full licence; and
    (b) have held that licence for at least 2 years; and
    (c) have a BAC not exceeding 0.05 while supervising.

Division 2 — Probationary Licences

Section 9 — Eligibility for probationary licence
    A person may apply for a probationary licence if the person—
    (a) has held a learner permit for at least 12 months; and
    (b) has completed a minimum of 120 hours of supervised driving,
        including at least 20 hours of night driving; and
    (c) has passed the prescribed driving test.

Section 10 — Conditions of probationary licence
    (1) A probationary licence is subject to the following conditions—
        (a) the holder must not have a BAC exceeding 0.00;
        (b) the holder must not drive a high-powered vehicle as
            prescribed in the regulations;
        (c) the holder must display P-plates in the prescribed manner.
    (2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence.
        Penalty: 15 penalty units.

PART 3 — DRINK AND DRUG DRIVING

Division 1 — Blood Alcohol Offences

Section 11 — Driving while exceeding prescribed BAC
    (1) A person must not drive a motor vehicle while the prescribed
        concentration of alcohol is present in the person's blood.
    (2) For the purposes of this section, the prescribed concentration
        of alcohol is—
        (a) for a person holding a learner permit or probationary
            licence—any concentration exceeding 0.00; or
        (b) for all other persons—a concentration exceeding 0.05.
    (3) Penalty—
        (a) for a first offence—25 penalty units or imprisonment for
            3 months, or both;
        (b) for a subsequent offence—120 penalty units or imprisonment
            for 12 months, or both.

Section 12 — Driving under influence of drugs
    (1) A person must not drive a motor vehicle while impaired by a drug.
    (2) For the purposes of this section, a prescribed drug includes—
        (a) methylamphetamine;
        (b) THC (delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol);
        (c) MDMA.
    (3) Penalty: 25 penalty units or imprisonment for 3 months, or both.

Division 2 — Testing Procedures

Section 13 — Preliminary breath test
    (1) A police officer may require any person driving or in charge of
        a motor vehicle on a highway to undergo a preliminary breath test.
    (2) A person must comply with a requirement under subsection (1).
        Penalty: 10 penalty units.
    (3) Evidence obtained under this section is admissible in proceedings
        under section 11.

Section 14 — Evidentiary breath analysis
    (1) If a preliminary breath test indicates the presence of alcohol,
        a police officer may require the person to undergo an evidentiary
        breath analysis at an approved testing station.
    (2) Cross-reference: see section 11 for prescribed BAC limits.

PART 4 — AUTOMATED VEHICLES

Section 15 — Registration of automated driving system
    (1) A person must not operate an automated driving system on a road
        unless the system is registered under this Part.
    (2) An application for registration must include—
        (a) evidence of compliance with the prescribed safety standards;
        (b) a safety management plan; and
        (c) evidence of insurance coverage as required by Part 6.
    (3) Penalty: 200 penalty units.

Section 16 — Duties of automated driving system entity
    (1) An ADSE has the duties of a driver when the automated driving
        system is engaged.
    (2) Cross-reference: for general driver duties, see Part 2.
    (3) The duties under the Workplace Health and Safety Act 2024
        continue to apply to an ADSE—see section 19 of that Act.

PART 5 — VEHICLE REGISTRATION

Section 17 — Requirement for registration
    (1) A person must not use an unregistered motor vehicle on a road.
        Penalty: 20 penalty units.
    (2) This section does not apply to a vehicle being driven directly to
        a place of inspection under section 18.

Section 18 — Roadworthiness inspections
    A motor vehicle must pass a roadworthiness inspection—
    (a) on initial registration; and
    (b) on transfer of registration; and
    (c) at intervals prescribed by the regulations.

PART 6 — INSURANCE

Section 19 — Compulsory third party insurance
    (1) The registered operator of a motor vehicle must maintain a
        policy of compulsory third party insurance in respect of the vehicle.
    (2) An insurer may refuse to issue a policy only on grounds
        prescribed by the regulations.
    (3) Cross-reference: for the interaction between CTP insurance and
        automated vehicles, see section 15(2)(c).

PART 7 — ENFORCEMENT AND PENALTIES

Section 20 — Powers of authorised officers
    An authorised officer may—
    (a) stop and inspect any motor vehicle on a road;
    (b) require the production of a licence or registration document;
    (c) issue an infringement notice for a prescribed offence.

Section 21 — Infringement penalties
    The following infringement penalties apply—
    (a) driving while unlicensed—5 penalty units;
    (b) driving an unregistered vehicle—10 penalty units;
    (c) failure to display P-plates or L-plates—3 penalty units;
    (d) exceeding speed limit by 10-25 km/h—5 penalty units;
    (e) exceeding speed limit by more than 25 km/h—15 penalty units.

Section 22 — Demerit points
    (1) A person incurs demerit points as prescribed in Schedule 1.
    (2) If a person accumulates 12 or more demerit points within a
        period of 3 years, the person's licence is suspended.
    (3) Cross-reference: for licence suspension procedures, see section 23.

Section 23 — Licence suspension and cancellation
    (1) The licensing authority may suspend or cancel a licence if—
        (a) the holder has accumulated excessive demerit points
            under section 22; or
        (b) the holder has been convicted of an offence under Part 3; or
        (c) the holder is no longer a fit and proper person.
    (2) A person whose licence is suspended must surrender the licence
        within 7 days.
        Penalty: 10 penalty units.

PART 8 — MISCELLANEOUS

Section 24 — Regulations
    The Governor in Council may make regulations for or with respect to—
    (a) prescribing forms, fees, and procedures;
    (b) prescribing standards for automated driving systems;
    (c) prescribing the manner of displaying number plates;
    (d) any matter necessary to give effect to this Act.

Section 25 — Savings and transitional provisions
    (1) A licence or permit in force under the repealed Road Safety
        Act 1986 continues in force as if issued under this Act.
    (2) Demerit points recorded before commencement are carried forward.

Section 26 — Repeal
    The Road Safety Act 1986 is repealed.
