PRIVACY ACT 2024

No. 15 of 2024

An Act to protect the privacy of individuals with respect to their
personal information, to regulate the collection, use, and disclosure
of personal information, and for related purposes.

The Parliament of Australia enacts:

PART 1 — PRELIMINARY

Section 1 — Short title
    This Act may be cited as the Privacy Act 2024.

Section 2 — Commencement
    This Act commences on the day after Royal Assent.

Section 3 — Objects
    The objects of this Act are—
    (a) to promote the protection of the privacy of individuals; and
    (b) to provide the basis for nationally consistent regulation of
        privacy and the handling of personal information; and
    (c) to promote responsible and transparent handling of personal
        information; and
    (d) to facilitate the free flow of information across national
        borders while ensuring that the privacy of individuals is
        respected; and
    (e) to provide a framework for the protection of children's
        personal information online.

Section 4 — Definitions
    In this Act—
    "APP" means an Australian Privacy Principle set out in Schedule 1;
    "APP entity" means an agency or organisation;
    "data breach" has the meaning given by section 26;
    "eligible data breach" has the meaning given by section 27;
    "overseas recipient" means a person who receives personal information
        and is not in Australia or an external Territory;
    "personal information" means information or an opinion about an
        identified individual, or an individual who is reasonably
        identifiable, whether the information or opinion is true or not;
    "sensitive information" means personal information about an
        individual's racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious
        beliefs, sexual orientation, criminal record, health information,
        genetic information, or biometric information.

PART 2 — AUSTRALIAN PRIVACY PRINCIPLES

Division 1 — Collection of Personal Information

Section 5 — APP 1: Open and transparent management
    An APP entity must take reasonable steps to implement practices,
    procedures, and systems that ensure compliance with the APPs and
    any registered APP code that binds the entity.

Section 6 — APP 3: Collection of solicited personal information
    (1) An APP entity must not collect personal information unless the
        information is reasonably necessary for one or more of the
        entity's functions or activities.
    (2) An APP entity must collect sensitive information only if—
        (a) the individual consents to the collection; and
        (b) the information is reasonably necessary for the entity's
            functions or activities.
    (3) Cross-reference: for notification requirements on collection,
        see section 7 (APP 5).

Section 7 — APP 5: Notification of collection
    (1) At or before the time an APP entity collects personal information,
        the entity must notify the individual of—
        (a) the identity and contact details of the entity;
        (b) the purposes of collection;
        (c) the consequences if information is not collected;
        (d) any other entities to which information may be disclosed;
        (e) the entity's privacy policy and how to access it.
    (2) The notification must be provided in a clear, current,
        understandable, and accessible form.

Division 2 — Use and Disclosure

Section 8 — APP 6: Use or disclosure of personal information
    (1) An APP entity may use or disclose personal information only for
        the primary purpose for which it was collected.
    (2) An APP entity may use or disclose personal information for a
        secondary purpose if—
        (a) the individual has consented; or
        (b) the individual would reasonably expect the use or disclosure; or
        (c) it is required or authorised by law.

Section 9 — APP 7: Direct marketing
    (1) An APP entity must not use personal information for direct
        marketing unless—
        (a) the individual has consented; or
        (b) it is impracticable to obtain consent and the individual has
            not requested to opt out.
    (2) An individual may opt out of direct marketing at any time.

Division 3 — Cross-border Disclosure

Section 10 — APP 8: Cross-border disclosure of personal information
    (1) Before disclosing personal information to an overseas recipient,
        an APP entity must take reasonable steps to ensure that the
        recipient does not breach the APPs.
    (2) An APP entity is not required to comply with subsection (1) if—
        (a) the entity reasonably believes the recipient is subject to a
            law or binding scheme that is substantially similar to the
            APPs; or
        (b) the individual has consented after being expressly informed
            that subsection (1) will not apply; or
        (c) the disclosure is required by Australian law.
    (3) An APP entity that discloses personal information under
        subsection (1) is accountable for any breach by the overseas
        recipient as if the breach were committed by the entity itself.
    (4) Cross-reference: for cross-border enforcement cooperation, see
        Part 5 of this Act.

Section 11 — Prescribed countries
    The Minister may, by legislative instrument, prescribe countries that
    have privacy protections substantially similar to the APPs for the
    purposes of section 10(2)(a).

PART 3 — NOTIFIABLE DATA BREACHES

Section 12 — Application of this Part
    This Part applies to an APP entity that holds personal information.

Section 13 — Meaning of data breach
    A data breach occurs when—
    (a) there is unauthorised access to personal information held by
        an entity; or
    (b) there is unauthorised disclosure of personal information held by
        an entity; or
    (c) personal information held by an entity is lost in circumstances
        where unauthorised access or disclosure is likely.

Section 14 — Meaning of eligible data breach
    (1) A data breach is an eligible data breach if a reasonable person
        would conclude that the breach is likely to result in serious
        harm to any of the individuals to whom the information relates.
    (2) In assessing serious harm, regard must be had to—
        (a) the kind of information involved;
        (b) the sensitivity of the information;
        (c) whether the information is protected by security measures;
        (d) the nature of the harm that may result.

Section 15 — Duty to notify Commissioner
    (1) If an APP entity has reasonable grounds to believe that an
        eligible data breach has occurred, the entity must notify the
        Information Commissioner as soon as practicable and in any
        case within 72 hours of becoming aware.
    (2) The notification must include—
        (a) a description of the breach;
        (b) the kind of information involved;
        (c) steps taken to contain the breach.
    (3) Penalty: 2,500 penalty units for a body corporate;
        500 penalty units for an individual.

Section 16 — Duty to notify affected individuals
    (1) If an entity notifies the Commissioner under section 15, the
        entity must also notify each affected individual.
    (2) The notification must include—
        (a) a description of the breach;
        (b) recommendations about steps the individual should take.
    (3) Cross-reference: see section 14 for the test of serious harm.

PART 4 — ENFORCEMENT

Section 17 — Civil penalty provisions
    (1) A serious or repeated interference with the privacy of an
        individual is a civil penalty provision.
    (2) Maximum penalty—
        (a) for a body corporate—the greater of:
            (i) 50,000 penalty units; or
            (ii) 3 times the benefit obtained from the contravention; or
            (iii) 30% of adjusted turnover during the breach period;
        (b) for an individual—2,500 penalty units.

Section 18 — Commissioner's powers
    The Information Commissioner may—
    (a) conduct investigations into breaches of the APPs;
    (b) accept enforceable undertakings;
    (c) seek civil penalties through the Federal Court;
    (d) issue infringement notices.

PART 5 — MISCELLANEOUS

Section 19 — Interaction with State and Territory laws
    This Act is not intended to exclude or limit the operation of a
    law of a State or Territory that is capable of operating
    concurrently with this Act.

Section 20 — Regulations
    The Governor-General may make regulations prescribing matters
    required or permitted by this Act to be prescribed.
